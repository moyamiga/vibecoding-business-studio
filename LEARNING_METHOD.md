# VibeCoding Business Studio Learning Method

## Purpose

VibeCoding Business Studio is a project-based learning method for people who
want to build real software with AI while developing judgment, execution,
business awareness, teamwork habits, and responsible AI literacy.

The project may be a website, application, automation, internal tool, campaign,
data project, game, or another digital outcome. The type of software is
secondary. The learning method is the product.

## Learning Outcome

A successful learner should be increasingly able to:

1. describe a user and problem;
2. reduce a large idea to a testable milestone;
3. define what “done” means;
4. use AI to accelerate work without surrendering decisions;
5. verify output with observable evidence;
6. connect technical choices to product and business consequences;
7. explain what was learned and what remains unclear.

## Roles

### Learner Product Owner

The learner owns:

- the project idea;
- the primary user;
- priorities;
- scope acceptance;
- product tradeoffs;
- milestone acceptance;
- explanations of what was learned.

AI should not silently make these decisions.

### Mentor or Supervisor

A teacher, parent, program leader, or other responsible adult owns or reviews
high-risk decisions involving:

- money;
- publication;
- privacy;
- legal obligations;
- identifying data about minors;
- external accounts;
- irreversible changes;
- access by teammates or third parties.

### AI Build Partner

Codex or another approved AI tool may act as:

- programmer;
- debugger;
- explainer;
- documentation assistant;
- product coach;
- project coordinator.

It should make progress and assumptions visible, preserve scope, run checks, and
return decisions to the learner when appropriate.

### Teammates

When a project has multiple learners, each person should have a written area of
ownership. Cross-area changes should be discussed rather than silently merged.

## Six-Stage Learning Loop

### 1. Idea

The learner describes:

- what they want to build;
- who it is for;
- what problem currently exists;
- why the project matters personally;
- how much time is available;
- their current experience.

The output is a project brief, not a technology stack.

### 2. Reality Check

The learner, mentor, and AI reduce the idea to a first milestone.

The output includes:

- a one-sentence product;
- a challenge to the current scope;
- one first milestone;
- work included now;
- work explicitly postponed;
- evidence of success;
- one business question;
- one AI-literacy question;
- risks that need supervision.

Postponing work is treated as a decision, not a failure.

### 3. Milestones

The project is divided into stages with:

- an objective;
- a definition of done;
- a short task list;
- a learning concept;
- a product question.

A milestone should create an observable result, not merely produce documents.

### 4. Guided Build Session

Before asking Codex to implement, the learner records:

- the milestone;
- the decision they own;
- what Codex should change;
- what remains outside scope;
- expected verification evidence.

Codex should then:

1. summarize its understanding;
2. identify contradictions;
3. propose the smallest verifiable change;
4. name files before changing them;
5. stop for high-impact decisions;
6. run available checks;
7. report evidence, assumptions, risks, and an understanding question.

### 5. Reflection

The learner explains:

- what was built;
- what decision they made;
- how they know it works;
- what product or business lesson appeared;
- what they understand now;
- what remains unclear;
- the next small milestone.

The learner does not need to memorize every line of code. They must be able to
explain the decisions, evidence, and limits.

### 6. Evidence

The learning report records:

- the original context;
- the scope decision;
- milestone progress;
- build evidence;
- reflection;
- AI use;
- the next step.

The report supports mentor feedback, portfolios, assessment, and learner
self-awareness.

## Learning Pillars

### Product Judgment

Questions:

- Who is the user?
- What problem exists without the proposed product?
- What is the smallest proof?
- What can wait?
- What would make the user return?
- What evidence would change the next decision?

### AI Literacy

Questions:

- What context did the AI receive?
- What assumptions did it make?
- What was inspected?
- What was tested?
- What was accepted without verification?
- Which decisions should remain human?

### Execution

Habits:

- work in small milestones;
- define acceptance before implementation;
- finish one workflow before adding features;
- run checks;
- record evidence;
- make the next action explicit.

### Business Awareness

A technical decision may affect:

- acquisition;
- retention;
- cost;
- support;
- maintenance;
- pricing;
- privacy;
- risk;
- competition;
- team incentives.

The method introduces one relevant business question at a time rather than
turning the project into an abstract business course.

### Reflection and Ownership

The learner should distinguish:

- output produced by AI;
- decisions made by the learner;
- evidence observed by a human;
- concepts understood;
- uncertainty that remains.

## Assessment Rubric

Each dimension may be assessed from 1 to 4.

### Product

1. The idea is a feature list.
2. A user and problem are named.
3. Scope and evidence are clear.
4. Decisions change in response to user evidence.

### Execution

1. Work is unstructured.
2. Tasks exist but “done” is unclear.
3. Milestones have definitions and checks.
4. The learner consistently ships and reflects on small increments.

### AI Literacy

1. AI output is accepted automatically.
2. The learner can describe what the AI produced.
3. The learner verifies output and identifies assumptions.
4. The learner chooses when to use AI, when to inspect, and when to escalate.

### Business Judgment

1. Business consequences are absent.
2. The learner can name a customer or source of value.
3. The learner connects a technical choice to cost, adoption, retention, or risk.
4. The learner uses evidence to compare meaningful tradeoffs.

### Communication and Reflection

1. The learner cannot explain what changed.
2. The learner can describe the result.
3. The learner can explain decisions and verification.
4. The learner can teach the tradeoff, state uncertainty, and propose the next
   experiment.

## Safety Rules

- Never expose credentials or private chats.
- Never publish identifying information about minors.
- Use adult or mentor approval for high-risk decisions.
- Do not misrepresent AI output as learner understanding.
- Avoid unlicensed assets or copied work.
- Make monetization discussions age-appropriate.
- Treat health, legal, financial, and other high-stakes projects as supervised
  learning exercises, not professional advice.

## Repeatable Output

Each project should produce:

- a project brief;
- a scope decision;
- a milestone plan;
- a working or testable artifact;
- verification evidence;
- a reflection;
- a learning report;
- a next-step decision.
